# Scot

Scot appears in the electronic editor’s account of the transmission of the Pseudomonarchia through Discoverie of Witchcraft. The discussion compares readings and describes the relationship of that text to the later Goetia catalogue.

## External historical context

**Scot is Reginald Scot**, whose *Discoverie of Witchcraft* appeared in **1584**. The Library of Congress preserves a first edition and describes his sceptical purpose in exposing supposed witchcraft and deceptive practices. Inclusion of magical material is therefore not, by itself, evidence that he endorsed or performed it. [Library of Congress account of its 1584 copy](https://blogs.loc.gov/loc/2020/10/that-magical-mystical-book-on-witchcraft-from-1584/).

An English version of [[Wier (Goetia)|Weyer's]] spirit catalogue appeared in this book; that textual connection explains Scot's importance to the Goetia. It does not identify him as the compiler of the later *Lemegeton*. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=91|Transmission discussion, PDF 91]]; [[Goetia Historical Context]].
