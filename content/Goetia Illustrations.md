# Goetia illustrations

These images reproduce artwork in the supplied [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf|Goetia PDF]]. The collection includes the seals printed alongside all **72 numbered spirits**, the ritual diagrams and vessels, the comparative drawings, and the uncertain seal attributed to [[Halahel (Goetia)|Halahel]]. The individual spirit notes embed their own images; use [[Goetia Entity Index]] to browse them.

A seal functions as an identifying emblem in this text. The spirits' bodily appearances are described in prose. The circle's colour scheme and layout belong to the supplied edition: its electronic editor explicitly says that the circle and triangle were redrawn and coloured. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=95|PDF 95]].

## Circle and triangle

![[Attachments/Diagrams/magical-circle-and-triangle.png|650]]

The complete plate from [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=53|PDF 53]]. The circle represents the operator's protected place; the triangle is the designated place for the appearing spirit. The surrounding divine and angelic names form part of the text's claimed authority and protection. The instructions and explanation are on [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=52|PDF 52]], with names indexed in [[Goetia Angels and Orders]] and [[Goetia Divine Names]].

![[Attachments/Diagrams/magical-triangle.png|350]]

Detail of the triangle from the same plate. [[Michael (Goetia)|Michael]]'s name is distributed around the green centre; [[Anaphaxeton (Goetia)|Anaphaxeton]], [[Tetragrammaton (Goetia)|Tetragrammaton]], and [[Primeumaton (Goetia)|Primeumaton]] surround the triangle. This is the book's spatial arrangement of invoked authority, rather than a picture of Michael's appearance.

## Comparative figures 162–174

The translator compares these drawings with the central human-like motif of the secret seal. The notes describe raised arms, crescents, caducei, and related forms on stelae or funerary objects. Their proposed resemblance is an editorial comparison; the figures are not additional members of the spirit catalogue. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=57|PDF 57]].

## Source-layout cautions

- **[[Leraje]], PDF 31:** a seal labelled **Beleth** sits above the clearly labelled Leraje seal, with an “or” between them, at the boundary with the preceding entry's continuation. It repeats [[Beleth]]'s second seal from PDF 30. The wiki displays both pieces of source evidence but does not resolve the uncertain placement into a verified second Leraje seal. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=30|PDF 30]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=31|PDF 31]].
- **[[Decarbia]], PDF 47:** the seal printed beside this entry is labelled **Seere** and matches [[Seere]]'s second seal on PDF 48. The image is retained with the mismatch stated explicitly. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=47|PDF 47]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=48|PDF 48]].
- The electronic editor discusses eight entries having two printed seal forms and warns that some seals were squeezed or stretched to fit circular borders. The wiki retains the supplied shapes and the forms' order rather than choosing a supposedly definitive replacement. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=92|PDF 92]].

## Comparative museum photographs

Two separately sourced photographs accompany the ritual-name notes:

- [[Agla (Goetia)|Agla]]: an English amuletic brooch, thirteenth century, bearing the formula.
- [[Emmanuel (Goetia)|Emmanuel]]: a Russian *Christ Emmanuel* icon, about 1775, illustrating the name's Christian use.

These are contextual images from The Walters Art Museum, not illustrations printed in the Goetia or evidence of its rituals being performed. Each note gives the museum record, image credit, and licence. Their [[Attachments/Comparative/provenance.json|separate provenance manifest]] records both downloads.

## Image provenance

For a historical object bearing one of the invoked names, see [[Agla (Goetia)|Agla's comparative medieval brooch]]. That photograph comes from a separate museum collection and has its own [[Attachments/Comparative/provenance.json|provenance record]]; it is not an illustration from this PDF.

The attachments contain **104 PNG files**: 80 catalogue seal placements, 10 ritual-diagram/talisman/vessel views, 13 comparative figures, and Halahel's seal. The catalogue count includes the two attribution problems described above. Every image has a source page, extraction method, and coordinates recorded in [[Attachments/illustration-provenance.json|the provenance manifest]], together with the source PDF's SHA-256 digest.

Font-based seals and composite drawings were rendered from their PDF regions at 288 dpi. Embedded bitmap illustrations retain their source pixels and the orientation specified by the PDF's placement matrix. The circle plate uses Poppler because MuPDF misrenders its rotated lettering. No missing linework, names, or letters have been reconstructed. See [[Goetia Source Guide]] for the edition's broader textual history.
