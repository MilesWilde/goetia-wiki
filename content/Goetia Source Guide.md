# Goetia source guide

This reference extracts the entities described in [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf|*Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*]]. It covers all **72 numbered spirits**, then groups the other named beings, celestial orders, divine names, and narrative figures found in this edition. The [[Goetia Conjurations|ritual guides]], [[Goetia Historical Context|historical timeline]], and [[Goetia Ritual Names|name glossary]] explain the larger sections and their context. Descriptions report what the text attributes to them; they do not establish independently verified supernatural facts.

## Which book?

The PDF reproduces the **1904 [[Mathers (Goetia)|Mathers]]–[[Aleister Crowley (Goetia)|Crowley]] edition**, reset as a **2003 Celephaïs Press electronic edition**. Its title page credits [[Aleister Crowley (Goetia)|Crowley]] as editor; the electronic notes identify [[Mathers (Goetia)|Mathers]] as the translator behind the initials [[Mathers (Goetia)|D.D.C.F.]] The file has **98 PDF pages**. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=5|Title page, PDF 5]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=87|edition notes, PDF 87–88]].

This is the first book of the *Lemegeton*, with introductions, ritual additions, marginalia, and later editorial notes. It is not a complete edition of all the *Lemegeton*'s books. The introduction describes *Theurgia-Goetia*, *Ars Paulina*, *Ars Almadel*, and the notary material, but their complete catalogues are absent here. Consequently, this wiki does not invent entries for their unnamed angels or import lists from other editions. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=22|Introduction, PDF 22–24]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=91|electronic notes, PDF 91–92]].

## Reading the evidence

| Layer | How these pages use it |
|---|---|
| Numbered spirit descriptions | Main evidence for each spirit's number, rank, appearance, offices, and legions |
| Ritual text and diagrams | Evidence for other beings, divine titles, relationships, and ceremonial vocabulary |
| Translator's notes | Labelled alternatives, manuscript readings, and uncertainty |
| [[Aleister Crowley (Goetia)\|Crowley]]'s commentary and marginalia | Attributed interpretations; the PDF often marks marginalia with double brackets |
| Electronic editor's notes | Later comparisons, corrections, and historical explanations; not silently substituted for the catalogue |
| External contextual sources | Explicitly cited biblical, linguistic, manuscript, publication, and museum evidence; separated from what this PDF itself states |
| Wiki subject groups | Navigation categories made for this reference, not a taxonomy asserted by the book |

The electronic editor explains the use of facsimiles and marginalia and warns about omissions, corrupt spellings, and variant readings. A spell's unusual word is not automatically the name of a distinct being. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=87|Editorial method, PDF 87]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=89|invocation spellings, PDF 89–91]].

## Names and disagreements

Pages retain this edition's names, including **[[Decarbia]]**, **[[Oriax]]**, **[[Cimejes]]**, and **[[Camio]]**. Alternative spellings actually present in the supplied text appear on the relevant profiles and in the master index. Similar names are not automatically merged: the four-quarter lists, for example, differ between passages.

Ranks follow individual catalogue descriptions. [[Ipos]] and [[Murmur]] are each called an **Earl** there, although the later classified list omits that title for them. The [[Goetia Ranks|rank index]] preserves both titles and explains the discrepancy. Superscript footnote numbers are separated from legion counts; uncertain or absent counts remain uncertain or absent.

## Context beyond the catalogue

The supplementary indexes distinguish named beings from collective orders, divine epithets, places, ritual speech, and people. A ritual name can have an explanatory note without becoming an additional spirit. [[Emmanuel (Goetia)|Emmanuel]], for example, needs biblical and ritual context even though the catalogue supplies no separate appearance or biography. [[Goetia Ritual Names]] also records expressions whose derivation remains uncertain.

The [[Goetia Historical Context|timeline]] distinguishes a text's legendary setting, surviving manuscript dates, publication dates, and claimed ritual practice. The conjurations are scripts for a prospective operator, not reports that identify the date and participants of each performance. External sources are linked beside the contextual claims they support. A correction to this edition is labelled as a correction, preserving its original wording as evidence.

## Illustrations and their limits

[[Goetia Illustrations]] gathers **104 extracts from this PDF**, with seals embedded on the relevant spirit profiles and diagrams accompanying the ritual explanations. Captions identify the source pages and explain intended functions. The [[Attachments/illustration-provenance.json|image manifest]] records extraction coordinates, methods, and the PDF digest. Images retain this edition's linework, placement, and attribution problems; the wiki does not redraw missing details or replace uncertain seals with modern alternatives.

External comparative artwork is labelled separately. The medieval brooch on [[Agla (Goetia)|Agla]] illustrates use of that formula on an actual object, while the eighteenth-century icon on [[Emmanuel (Goetia)|Emmanuel]] shows the name's Christian use. Neither is an illustration from the Goetia. Museum records, photographer credits, licences, and [[Attachments/Comparative/provenance.json|separate provenance]] accompany the images.
