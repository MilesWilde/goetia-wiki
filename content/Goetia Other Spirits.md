# Other spirits and infernal hierarchies

The book mentions beings beyond its 72 numbered profiles. Some govern other spirits, some appear only in comparisons or editorial notes, and some names belong to incompatible lists. They are grouped here by their actual context.

## Kings of the quarters

The observations after the catalogue give **[[Amaymon (Goetia)|Amaymon]], [[Corson (Goetia)|Corson]], [[Zimimay (Goetia)|Zimimay]] or [[Zimimay (Goetia)|Ziminiar]], and [[Göap (Goetia)|Göap]]** as four great kings governing the cardinal directions. That passage does **not** assign an individual direction to each king. Its footnote supplies two alternative sets of names. These lists are retained together without inventing one-to-one identifications. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=49|PDF 49, printed 35]].

The later Angelic-language invocation gives **[[Gäap|Gaap]], [[Paimon]], or [[Zimimay (Goetia)|Zodimay]]** as substitutions for [[Amaymon (Goetia)|Amaimon]] and offers alternate directional words. It does not provide a reliable complete correspondence table. The electronic editor explicitly observes that most spirits' quarter assignments are unstated. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=80|PDF 80]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=94|94]].

### Two different groups of four

The **Four Great Princes** of the polemical preface are not named there. The electronic editor suggests **[[Lucifer (Goetia)|Lucifer]], [[Leviathan (Goetia)|Leviathan]], [[Satan (Goetia)|Satan]], and [[Belial]]**, referring to *Abramelin*. That is a later identification, distinct from the quarter kings above. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=7|Preface, PDF 7]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=88|editor's interpretation, PDF 88]].

The vessel story names yet another group: **[[Belial]], [[Beleth|Bileth / Beleth]], [[Asmoday]], and [[Gäap|Gaap / Gäap]]** are chief among the spirits confined by [[Solomon (Goetia)|Solomon]]. Following their release, [[Belial]] is said to enter an image and answer those who worship it. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=49|PDF 49]].

## Collective beings and ambiguous names

| Term | What the source supports |
|---|---|
| **Princes, Genii, Liachidæ, ministers of the Tartarean abode** | Collective authorities invoked in the first conjuration, not individually profiled spirits. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=60\|PDF 60]] |
| **Chief Prince of the Seat of Apologia in the Ninth Legion** | An unnamed authority in that same invocation. The text does not identify him as one of the 72. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=60\|PDF 60]] |
| **Beralanensis, Baldachiensis, Paumachia, Apologiæ Sedes** | Command names/formulas attached to those authorities. Their grammar does not establish four separate biographies. Also indexed under [[Goetia Divine Names\|divine and ritual names]]. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=60\|PDF 60]] |
| **Familiars, legions, dead souls, and attending spirits** | Recurring groups in individual profiles. Their powers belong to the stated spirit's entry; unnamed members receive no invented names. Browse [[Goetia Offices and Motifs\|offices and motifs]]. |
| **Theurgia-Goetia's mixed spirits** | Another book's inhabitants, described collectively in the introduction and compared with [[Halahel (Goetia)\|Halahel]]. This edition does not contain their full catalogue. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=22\|PDF 22]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=59\|59]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=91\|91]] |
| **Abaddon** | Used as a depth or place of confinement in the Angelic-language conjurations, rather than given the profile of an individual angel. The corresponding English Constraint says Bottomless Pit or Abyss. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=63\|PDF 63]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=80\|80]] |
