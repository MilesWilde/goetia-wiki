# Cimejes

According to the *Goëtia*, Cimejes teaches grammar, logic, and rhetoric and discovers lost or hidden things and treasure.

| Field | Catalogue record |

| Rank(s) | Marquis |

| Legions | 30 |

## Appearance

Cimejes appears as a valiant warrior mounted on a fine black horse. The catalogue describes him with the rank of Marquis and emphasizes his might, strength, and power.

## Offices and powers

His teaching covers grammar, logic, and rhetoric. He also discovers lost or hidden things and treasures. In addition to a count of thirty legions, the entry gives him a regional authority: rule over all spirits in the parts of Africa.

## Relationships and textual notes

The numbered heading supplies Cimejes, Cimeies, and Kimaris. The classified rank list uses Cimeies, and the Hebrew-name table is headed Kimaris. [[Aleister Crowley (Goetia)|Crowley]]’s introductory essay uses Cimieries when discussing the teaching of logic; this spelling belongs to that interpretive essay rather than the numbered description. The warrior and horse are details of his manifestation; the enumerated offices are instruction and discovery. His claim to rule spirits in Africa is stated explicitly in the source and is retained as the book’s geographical attribution.
