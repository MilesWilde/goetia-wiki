# Infernal Source Guide

This collection describes demons and named supernatural beings in **J. Collin de Plancy's _Dictionnaire infernal_, sixth edition, Paris, Henri Plon, 1863**. It uses English descriptions and English name forms where the identification is clear, while retaining the edition's French headwords as searchable source names. The [[sources/dictionnaireinfernal.pdf#page=10|title page]] and [BnF catalogue record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb36576801h) establish the edition.

## What kind of source is this?

The book is a broad alphabetical dictionary of beliefs, reported marvels, people, books, practices, objects, and supernatural figures. Its contents extend well beyond a demon catalogue. The original title includes spirits, demons, sorcerers, divination, magic, superstition, and contemporary spiritualism. This collection selects the named supernatural beings from that wider work. [[sources/dictionnaireinfernal.pdf#page=10|Title page, PDF 10]]; [[sources/dictionnaireinfernal.pdf#page=12|preface, PDF 12–13]].

The sixth edition speaks from an expressly **Catholic apologetic viewpoint**. In the preface, Collin de Plancy criticizes his own earlier editions of 1818 and 1825 and describes a change of religious outlook in 1841. The approval preceding the title page is dated 26 December 1862. These passages explain the perspective of this edition; they do not make its historical anecdotes or judgments independently established facts. [[sources/dictionnaireinfernal.pdf#page=7|Approval, PDF 7]]; [[sources/dictionnaireinfernal.pdf#page=12|preface, PDF 12]].

Descriptions distinguish the dictionary's claims from modern editorial explanations. In particular, when Collin de Plancy calls a figure from another religious tradition a demon or devil, that classification is attributed to him. A person's accusation, confession, possession narrative, magical reputation, or appearance in the dictionary does not by itself make that person a supernatural being.

## Illustrations

Both the visually checked [[sources/dictionnaireinfernal.pdf#page=10|title page, PDF 10]] and [[sources/dictionnaireinfernal.pdf#page=13|preface, PDF 13]] advertise **550 engravings, including 72 demon portraits**, credited to **L. Breton**. The title-page OCR incorrectly reads the first total as 500. The visible printing reads 550. These are the edition's advertised totals, not an independently verified count of extracted assets.

The 72 advertised portraits do not define a roster identical to the Goetia's 72 numbered spirits. Many other illustrations depict ordinary animals, objects, people, or narrative scenes. A picture's location near a name does not establish that it is that figure's portrait. Captions, entry boundaries, and neighboring text must establish the association.

Image captions identify their source page, preserve source attribution, and distinguish a named portrait from a general narrative illustration. Crops preserve the scan's original linework and artist signatures where present. The provenance manifest records the source, page, crop coordinates, and extraction method.

## Relationship to other sources

Each profile describes this edition's account. A link to a Goetia profile is an explicit comparison, not permission to copy ranks, numbers, powers, spellings, appearances, or relationships between the two sources. Preserve disagreements and explain uncertain identifications. English prose may summarize the French, but a helpful translation must not quietly add information absent from the cited passage.

## Reading the scan

The PDF contains searchable OCR over page images. OCR can confuse names, numerals, accents, column order, and picture captions. Use it to find passages; confirm consequential readings against the visible page. Short cross-references such as _Voy._ point to another article and are not independent biographies. A coverage inventory records which source entries have been checked, which names are aliases or cross-references, which candidates are excluded, and which evidence comes from the two-page supplement.
