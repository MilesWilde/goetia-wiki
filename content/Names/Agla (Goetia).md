# Agla

**Agla is a compressed devotional formula used here as a divine name of command.**

## In this edition

The [[Goetia Second Conjuration|second conjuration]] calls Agla a name of [[God (Goetia)|God]] and associates it with the rescue of [[Lot (Goetia)|Lot]] and his family. It then appears again alongside On and [[Tetragrammaton (Goetia)|Tetragrammaton]] among the names by which the spirit is constrained. The book gives no separate Agla biography, appearance, or rank. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|PDF 61]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=62|PDF 62]].

## Historical context

The Walters Art Museum identifies AGLA as the initials of **Atah Gibor Le'olam [[Adonai (Goetia)|Adonai]]**, a Hebrew praise of the Lord's enduring might. A thirteenth-century English brooch bears the abbreviation: the expression had a devotional and amuletic life outside this Goetic text. [Walters Art Museum, Amuletic Brooch, 57.1987](https://art.thewalters.org/object/57.1987/).

This helps explain why a string that looks like an unfamiliar person's name can instead be an entire prayer condensed into letters. It does not establish the grimoire's claim that Lot spoke that abbreviation.
