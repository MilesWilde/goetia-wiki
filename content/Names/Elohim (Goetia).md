# Elohim

**Elohim is used as a divine name, but the circle also uses the same word for an angelic order. Context matters.**

## In this edition

The [[Goetia First Conjuration|first conjuration]] names Elohim and Elohi in its appeal to [[God (Goetia)|God]]. The circle has compounds including **[[Iehovah (Goetia)|Iehovah]] Elohim**, **Elohim Gibor**, and **Elohim [[Zabaoth (Goetia)|Tzabaoth]]**, each occupying the divine-name position in a different sequence. In the Netzach sequence, however, **Elohim** occupies the angelic-order position after [[Haniel (Goetia)|Haniel]]. See [[Goetia Angels and Orders]] for the complete arrangement. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|PDF 61]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=52|PDF 52]].

The later prayer repeatedly asks for divine presence beneath the heading Elohim. Its introductory note warns that the accompanying sentences are a paraphrased prayer rather than literal translations. Consequently the prayer's wording does not establish that Elohim means the same thing as [[Emmanuel (Goetia)|Emmanuel]]. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=69|PDF 69]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=70|PDF 70]].

## Historical and biblical context

Elohim has a plural grammatical form, but biblical usage can apply it to the singular God of Israel. [Jewish Encyclopedia, “God, Names of,” Elohim](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6727-god-names-of).
