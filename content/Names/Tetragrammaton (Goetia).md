# Tetragrammaton

**Tetragrammaton designates the four-letter divine name, YHWH.** The grimoire also treats the word itself as something to speak and inscribe. [Jewish Encyclopedia, “God, Names of”](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6727-god-names-of); [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=52|PDF 52]].

## In this edition

The [[Goetia First Conjuration|first conjuration]] combines Tetragrammaton with [[Iehovah (Goetia)|Iehovah]], describing a name before which the elements and celestial, earthly, and infernal powers tremble. The second joins it with [[Agla (Goetia)|Agla]] and On; the [[Goetia Constraint|Constraint]] includes it among seven names of binding. Its purpose is to establish authority over the summoned spirit. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|PDF 61]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=62|PDF 62]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=63|PDF 63]].

The circle instructions distribute its syllables across pentagrams. Later prayers associate it with the protection and presence of [[God (Goetia)|God]]. These uses explain why the same word appears in both the spoken conjurations and protective diagrams. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=52|PDF 52]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=70|PDF 70]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=71|PDF 71]].

## Historical and biblical context

The biblical name is distinguished from the title [[Adonai (Goetia)|Adonai]], which is used in its place when reading. Their connection does not make them separate gods. [USCCB, Exodus 3, note on 3:14](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/exodus/3).
