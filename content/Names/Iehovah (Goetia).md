# Iehovah

**Iehovah is a written form of the divine name used in the grimoire's commands, circle, and prayers.**

## In this edition

The [[Goetia First Conjuration|first conjuration]] joins it to [[Tetragrammaton (Goetia)|Tetragrammaton]] and describes the resulting divine name as terrifying to the powers of heaven, earth, and the infernal world. This is the authority by which the operator demands that the spirit appear and answer. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|PDF 61]].

The circle uses Iehovah within **Iehovah [[Elohim (Goetia)|Elohim]]**, **Iehovah Eloah va-Daath**, and **Iehovah [[Zabaoth (Goetia)|Tzabaoth]]**. Its later prayer repeatedly places petitions to [[God (Goetia)|God]] beneath the same name. These are different ritual contexts for a divine designation, not three newly described entities. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=52|PDF 52]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=69|PDF 69]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=70|PDF 70]].

## Historical and biblical context

The spelling corresponds to **Jehovah**, a historical reading that combined the consonants of YHWH with vowel signs associated with its substitute reading. It should not be presented as an independently established ancient pronunciation. [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Jehovah”](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/8568).
