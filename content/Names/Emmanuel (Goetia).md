# Emmanuel

**Emmanuel is a biblical name used here as an appeal to divine authority and protection.** It is not given an independent spirit profile, rank, legion count, or seal in this edition. It still deserves an explanatory entry: a name's role in a conjuration is useful information even when it is not a separate character.

## What it does in this book

The [[Goetia Second Conjuration]] associates Emmanuel with the deliverance of [[Shadrach (Goetia)|Shadrach]], [[Meschach (Goetia)|Meschach]], and [[Abed-nego (Goetia)|Abed-nego]] from the fiery furnace. The operator invokes that precedent while commanding an unnamed, selected spirit to appear and answer. Emmanuel belongs to the authority appealed to; the spirit being addressed occupies the placeholder **N.** The Angelic-language version retains the same name and story. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=62|PDF 62]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=78|PDF 78]].

The book gives no appearance or personal history for Emmanuel. That explains the difference from a page such as [[Paimon]], whose numbered entry supplies an appearance, offices, attendants, and legions. The earlier wiki listed Emmanuel only in the divine-name table; this note fills the explanatory gap without turning a title into an additional Goetic spirit.

## Biblical context

Matthew 1:23 explains Emmanuel as **“God is with us”** and applies the name to the birth of [[Jesus (Goetia)|Jesus]]. Matthew quotes the sign of the child in Isaiah 7:14. This is external biblical context for the name, not a definition supplied by the Goetia catalogue. [Matthew 1:20–25](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/matthew/1); [Isaiah 7:14 and its notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/isaiah/7).

**Immanuel** is another English spelling of the biblical name, used for example in the [ESV text of Matthew 1:23](https://www.esv.org/Matthew%2B1:23/). It is included as a lookup alias; this Goetia edition prints **Emmanuel**.

The furnace narrative is found in Daniel 3. The Goetia's claim that the three youths sang this particular name belongs to its own ritual retelling; it should not be mistaken for a direct quotation of that chapter or a dated record of an actual Goetic ceremony. [Daniel 3](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/daniel/3); [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=62|the book's wording, PDF 62]].

## What period is involved?

There are different dates to keep apart: the biblical stories invoked as precedents, the early modern compilation of the Goetia, and the 1904 edition reproduced in this PDF. A name's biblical origin does not date the surrounding English conjuration to biblical times. See [[Goetia Historical Context]] for the evidence and chronology.
