# El

**El functions here as a name of [[God (Goetia)|God]], lending authority to the operator's command.**

## In this edition

The [[Goetia First Conjuration|first conjuration]] explicitly calls El a powerful divine name and characterizes it with strength and wonder. It appears again within the longer sequence of names used to require a visible, intelligible, peaceful appearance from the spirit. This is an appeal to the creator's authority, rather than the introduction of another summoned being. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|PDF 61]].

In the circle, El begins the Chesed sequence, followed by [[Tzadquiel (Goetia)|Tzadquiel]], [[Goetia Angels and Orders#The magical circle|Chaschmalin]], and the sphere of Jupiter. The word also occurs within **[[Shaddai (Goetia)|Shaddaï el Chai]]** in the Yesod sequence. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=52|PDF 52]].

## Historical and biblical context

El is a Semitic word for deity. Its often-repeated derivation from a root meaning strength is uncertain. [Jewish Encyclopedia, “God, Names of,” El](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6727-god-names-of).

The conjuration's description of El as strong should therefore be reported as the book's language, rather than presented as a proven etymology.
