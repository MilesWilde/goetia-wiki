# Ararita

**Ararita is recorded here as an inscription on the vessel of brass.** It is not given an independent spirit profile.

## In this edition

The electronic editor transcribes the Hebrew band on the back of the vessel with **Ararita** followed by angelic names or orders. Two readings are supplied: one continues with [[Goetia Angels and Orders#The magical circle|Chashmalim]] and [[Tzadquiel (Goetia)|Tzadqiel]]; the other includes [[Raphael (Goetia)|Raphael]], [[Kamael (Goetia)|Kamael]], [[Tzadquiel (Goetia)|Tzadqiel]], and [[Tzaphquiel (Goetia)|Tzaphqiel]]. This establishes the word's place in a ritual object's inscription, but the passage does not translate it. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=95|PDF 95]].

## A later ceremonial comparison

In *Liber O*, published in *The Equinox* I.2 in **1909**, [[Aleister Crowley (Goetia)|Crowley]] uses Ararita in a hexagram ritual and explains it as the initials of a sentence affirming divine oneness in beginning, individuality, and transformation. [*Liber O*, section IV, hexagram ritual, printed p. 23](https://maestrosdelsaber.com/wp-content/uploads/ftp-files/Magia/a%20Crowley%2C%20Aleister/Mag%20Crowley%20The%20Equinox%20-%20The%20Equinnox%2C%20Vol%201%20No%202.pdf#page=35).

This is a documented later explanation in Crowley's ceremonial vocabulary. It is not a translation provided by the vessel passage, nor does it by itself date the word's first use.
