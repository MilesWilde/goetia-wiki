# Iah

**Iah is this edition's spelling of a short divine name.** Its separate note preserves the spelling and ritual setting without treating it as a separate god.

## In this edition

Iah appears among the divine names in the [[Goetia First Conjuration|first conjuration]]. The second pairs **Iah and Vau** and claims that [[Adam (Goetia)|Adam]] heard and spoke them. That attribution belongs to the grimoire's account of divine authority; it is not a dated record of Adam performing a Goetic rite. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|PDF 61]].

The circle's Chokmah sequence begins with Iah, followed by [[Ratziel (Goetia)|Ratziel]], [[Goetia Angels and Orders#The magical circle|Auphanim]], and the sphere of the fixed stars or zodiac. Here it occupies the divine-name position within a sequence of correspondences. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=52|PDF 52]].

## Historical and biblical context

Yah is a shortened form of YHWH. This supplies the context for the printed spelling Iah. [Jewish Encyclopedia, “God, Names of,” Yah](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6727-god-names-of).

The pairing with Vau does not supply Vau with a personality or biography. See [[Goetia Ritual Names]] for other words whose role is clearer than their full derivation.
