# Invocation of the King

This is an appeal to the chosen spirit's superior. Rather than asking the king to perform the original task, the operator demands that the king send the subordinate spirit and make it comply. The text offers this route when the earlier summonses fail because the subordinate may have been sent elsewhere. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=63|Reason for the appeal, PDF 63]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=64|Invocation, PDF 64, printed 48]].

## Which king?

The printed formula addresses **Amaimon**, a spelling connected in this wiki with [[Amaymon (Goetia)|Amaymon]], as a king of the east. A footnote says that the king should correspond to the relevant quarter of the world. This is a territorial or directional authority within the text's spirit hierarchy, not a human monarch attending the ceremony. The book's quarter-lists and spellings vary; see [[Goetia Other Spirits]] rather than assuming a single consistent list. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=64|Formula and quarter-note, PDF 64]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=80|Parallel version and variants, PDF 80–81]].

The speaker still claims authority above the king through [[God (Goetia)|God]], called El in this passage. N. continues to mean the particular subordinate the operator wants to summon. Neither N. nor “king” is an additional numbered entry in the catalogue. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=64|PDF 64]].

## Place in the sequence

After this appeal, the rubric returns to the original summonses; continued failure leads to the [[Goetia Spirits Chain|Spirits' Chain]]. Thus this is a conditional intervention in the same operation, not the opening of a separate story. It belongs to the early modern ritual layer described in [[Goetia Historical Context]]; the passage records no dated performance or identifiable human practitioner. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=64|Following rubric, PDF 64]].
