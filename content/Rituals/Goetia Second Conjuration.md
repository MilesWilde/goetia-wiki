# Second Conjuration

The second summons repeats the demand for a peaceful, visible appearance and truthful answers, but supports it with a long chain of biblical precedents. It is offered when the [[Goetia First Conjuration|First Conjuration]] has not secured the desired response. The intended interlocutors remain the human operator and the chosen spirit, represented by N. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|Transition and opening, PDF 61]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=62|Main formula, PDF 62, printed 46]].

## Why the biblical stories appear

The formula pairs sacred names with acts of deliverance, judgment, or mastery over nature. Its examples include [[Moses (Goetia)|Moses]] and the plagues, [[Joshua (Goetia)|Joshua]] and the sun, [[Daniel (Goetia)|Daniel]] and his deliverance, and [[Shadrach (Goetia)|Shadrach]], [[Meschach (Goetia)|Meschach]], and [[Abed-nego (Goetia)|Abed-nego]] in the furnace. Within the ritual's argument, the power that once accomplished those wonders is being invoked to compel the present spirit. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|Biblical examples, PDF 61–62]].

Those figures are **precedents invoked by the speaker**, not a gathering of people who participated in one ceremony. The text's assertion that they used particular names is also a grimoire claim; it should not automatically be treated as a quotation from the biblical account. In particular, [[Emmanuel (Goetia)|Emmanuel]] appears here as a sacred name associated with the furnace rescue. That explains why its role is different from the biographies and offices of the numbered spirits. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=62|Emmanuel and the furnace, PDF 62]].

## What changes from the first summons?

Threats of judgment and exclusion accompany the expanded appeal to divine authority. The desired result is still a controlled conversation and work within the chosen spirit's capacity. If this fails, the text next supplies the [[Goetia Constraint|Constraint]]. It does not say that every operation necessarily reaches that stage. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=62|Demands, PDF 62]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=63|Conditional transition, PDF 63]].

This is part of the early modern English ritual layer, transmitted here through the 1904 edition and its 2003 reset; see [[Goetia Historical Context]]. The electronic editor compares its wording with earlier ritual texts. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=96|Editorial comparisons, PDF 96]].
