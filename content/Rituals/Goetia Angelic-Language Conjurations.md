# Angelic-Language Conjurations

This substantial later section presents the ritual formulas in an “Angelic” language, with English meanings printed beneath them. It is a parallel recasting of the preparation, summonses, coercive stages, welcome, and dismissal. Its position after the English text does not mean the operator has begun a second round of new stages. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=73|Section title, PDF 73]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=75|Parallel text, PDF 75–85, printed 59–69]].

## Who composed this version?

The section's deliberately archaic title credits [[Perdurabo (Goetia)|Perdurabo]], the ritual name used by [[Aleister Crowley (Goetia)|Aleister Crowley]]. Its claim that [[Solomon (Goetia)|Solomon]] handed the material to [[Lemuel (Goetia)|Lemuel]] is part of the edition's legendary framing, not evidence for an ancient manuscript of these translations. The electronic editor identifies these formulas as part of the 1904 publication and explains how the 2003 reset reproduces them. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=73|Attribution, PDF 73]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=97|Editorial account, PDF 97, printed 81]].

## Where the language came from

The electronic editor connects the language, now commonly called **Enochian**, with the recorded scrying work of [[John Dee (Goetia)|John Dee]] and [[Edward Kelly (Goetia)|Edward Kelly]] in the **1580s**. In those reports Kelly served as the medium through whom Dee received material presented as angelic speech. That identifies the historical human participants and their claim; it does not independently verify the claimed angelic origin. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=97|Language history, PDF 97]].

Dee and Kelly were not the authors of the Goetia translations printed here. The available vocabulary was later reused to compose these new versions, with spelling conventions associated with the Golden Dawn. The editor also identifies suspected mistakes and unattested words. Consequently, an unfamiliar word in this section may be translated vocabulary, a spelling convention, or an error rather than another entity's name. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=97|Vocabulary and textual cautions, PDF 97]].
