# Preliminary Invocation

This opening prayer asks a supreme divine power, the [[Bornless One (Goetia)|Bornless One]], to give the speaker authority over spirits. It prepares the speaker's claimed spiritual standing before any particular catalogue spirit is addressed. The repeated request concerns spirits throughout the cosmos; it is not a summons to one of the seventy-two. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=9|Invocation, PDF 9–13, printed vii–xi]].

## What the language is doing

The speaker first praises the creator, adopts the voice of [[Moses (Goetia)|Mosheh]], and finally speaks as the invoked power itself. This movement from petition to identification explains the increasingly emphatic first-person declarations. Mosheh is a role claimed in the speech, not a named contemporary participant in a documented ceremony. The unusual strings of sacred words are part of the invocation; they do not each establish a separate entity with a recoverable biography. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=9|Opening identification, PDF 9]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=13|Closing declarations, PDF 13]].

## Which historical period?

The electronic editor traces this prayer to the *Stele of [[Jeu the Hieroglyphist (Goetia)|Jeu the Hieroglyphist]]*, PGM V 96–172, an ancient Greco-Egyptian exorcism. Its adaptation as a Goetia preliminary belongs to the modern edition. The editor suggests [[Allan Bennett (Goetia)|Allan Bennett]] may have made the adaptation, but does not establish that attribution. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=89|Electronic editor's source discussion, PDF 89–90]].

**External manuscript evidence:** the British Library dates the surviving Greek witness, Papyrus 46, to the **fourth century CE**, and locates the *Stele of Ieu* on folios 2r–3r. This dates the surviving papyrus, not the first composition of the prayer or a known performance. Its catalogue also records [[Charles Wycliffe Goodwin (Goetia)|Charles Wycliffe Goodwin]]'s 1852 publication. [British Library, Papyrus 46](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002104464).

The Egyptian identifications, elemental directions, and numerological glosses in double brackets are [[Aleister Crowley (Goetia)|Crowley]]'s marginal interpretation. They should not be projected back into either the ancient papyrus or the seventeenth-century English Goetia. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=9|Marginalia, PDF 9–13]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=89|Editorial explanation, PDF 89]].
