# Greater Curse

The Greater Curse is the final major escalation against a spirit that has not appeared. It threatens the loss of the spirit's rank, place, and happiness, followed by confinement and punishment until the last judgment. The appeal expands to heaven, the sun, moon, stars, and the wider creation as witnesses to the curse. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=66|Greater Curse, PDF 66, printed 50]].

## Purpose and participants

The human operator again claims authority derived from [[God (Goetia)|God]]. The chosen spirit, N., is the threatened party. Its written name or seal is treated as the material representative of that spirit, continuing the symbolism of the [[Goetia Conjuration of Fire|Conjuration of Fire]]. The celestial bodies are invoked within the rhetoric of universal judgment; the passage is not a catalogue of newly introduced gods. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=66|PDF 66]].

The coercion is meant to obtain an appearance and obedience. It is therefore conditional, rather than a punishment pursued regardless of outcome. A footnote records that some copies call this simply the “Curse.” [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=66|Text and title note, PDF 66]].

## Why the tone changes afterward

Once the spirit appears, the rubric changes from threats to relief and hospitality. The operator is told to end the punitive treatment and receive the spirit courteously. The following [[Goetia Address and Welcome|Address and Welcome]] maintains authority while moving toward a controlled exchange of requests and answers. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=66|Closing rubric, PDF 66–67]].

This transition is important to understanding the section: the book does not present one uninterrupted denunciation, and it does not require that every successful summons pass through all the curses. Its conditional directions are literary evidence for the intended procedure, not a record of who performed it or when. See [[Goetia Historical Context]].
