# The Spirits' Chain

The “General Curse” or “Spirits' Chain” is a stronger demand aimed at the spirit that has failed to appear. It threatens confinement in the abyss and punitive fire unless the spirit comes promptly and peacefully. Its target is the selected spirit N., not a named human enemy. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=64|Opening, PDF 64]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=65|Continuation, PDF 65, printed 49]].

## Why a chain?

The earlier rubric says this formula can break the chains supposedly preventing a spirit's arrival. The formula itself also threatens the disobedient spirit with binding. The same image therefore serves two purposes: releasing a spirit from an obstacle and threatening it with a new restraint. This tension is present in the text, rather than a reason to invent a separate entity called the Chain. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=63|Explanation of impediments, PDF 63]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=65|Threat of binding, PDF 65]].

The unnamed operator speaks as a representative of [[God (Goetia)|God]], while the spirit is portrayed as answerable to the creator despite its resistance. The threat is conditional: appearance and compliance are the intended way to end the punishment. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=64|PDF 64–65]].

## What follows?

If there is still no response, the rubric introduces a material representation of the spirit through its name or seal and moves toward the [[Goetia Conjuration of Fire|Conjuration of Fire]]. A later footnote also records the title “Lesser Curse” in some copies. These variants describe stages and manuscript terminology, not distinct beings. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=65|Transition, PDF 65]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=66|Title variants, PDF 66]].

For the early modern setting of this prescriptive text, and the distinction between a ritual script and a witnessed event, see [[Goetia Historical Context]].
