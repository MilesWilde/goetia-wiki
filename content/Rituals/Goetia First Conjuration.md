# First Conjuration

This is the initial summons to a chosen spirit from the [[Goetia Entity Index|catalogue]]. It asks the spirit to appear visibly, assume an approachable human form, speak intelligibly, and answer truthfully. **N. is a placeholder for that spirit's name**, not a mysterious additional entity. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=60|Opening and name-placeholder note, PDF 60]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|Requested appearance and answers, PDF 61, printed 45]].

## Why so many names?

The unnamed operator presents themself as made in the image of [[God (Goetia)|God]] and empowered by divine majesty. The long sequence of [[Goetia Divine Names|sacred names]], together with appeals to spiritual rulers and orders, builds the claimed authority behind the command. The argument is that the spirit must obey the higher power whose names the operator invokes. It is not a list of equally ranked spirits all being summoned together. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=60|Conjuration, PDF 60–61]].

Here, “conjure” means to solemnly charge or command through sacred authority. “Exorcise” is also used for this commanding action: in this passage the goal is to call a spirit into an appearance, rather than to describe the expulsion of a possessing spirit from a patient. This reading follows what the formula actually requests. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|PDF 61]].

## Place in the book

The rubric anticipates repetition and then offers the [[Goetia Second Conjuration|Second Conjuration]] if the spirit has not appeared. These are instructions for an imagined or intended operation, not minutes from a witnessed performance. The electronic editor identifies close parallels with the *Heptameron* and *Key of Solomon* tradition; the surviving English compilation belongs to the early modern history outlined in [[Goetia Historical Context]]. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|Transition, PDF 61]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=96|Editorial comparison, PDF 96]].
