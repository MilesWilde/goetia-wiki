# The Constraint

The Constraint is an escalation after the first two summonses fail. The speaker threatens the chosen spirit with loss of office, deprivation, and confinement until judgment, again claiming authority from [[God (Goetia)|God]]. The names said to have enabled [[Solomon (Goetia)|Solomon]] to bind spirits in the brass vessel become a precedent for the present demand. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=63|Constraint, PDF 63, printed 47]].

## What “constraint” means here

The aim is compulsion: the operator claims the right to punish refusal. This is a rhetorical and ceremonial assertion of superior jurisdiction over the spirit, not a separate creature or a description of a historical prison. The intended result remains a prompt, peaceful appearance that does not injure the operator. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=63|PDF 63]].

## The two explanations for failure

The following rubric proposes different reasons a spirit might not appear. If its king has dispatched it elsewhere, the operator is directed to the [[Goetia Invocation of the King|Invocation of the King]]. If it is bound in chains in hell and outside that king's custody, the relevant recourse is the [[Goetia Spirits Chain|Spirits' Chain]]. These alternatives show the ritual's own explanation of resistance and absence; they are not independent evidence that such circumstances occurred. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=63|Rubric after the Constraint, PDF 63]].

The human speaker and the chosen spirit are unnamed. Solomon supplies legendary authority, rather than an identifiable date or witness to a performance. This section belongs to the early modern English ritual material; [[Goetia Historical Context]] explains its transmission into this edition.
