# Licence to Depart

The Licence formally releases the spirit after the requested exchange. It acknowledges the spirit's response, authorises its return to its proper place, and requires a peaceful departure without harm to people or animals. It also preserves the operator's claim that the spirit should answer a future summons. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=68|Licence to Depart, PDF 68, printed 52]].

## Why the ritual needs an ending

The earlier [[Goetia Address and Welcome|Welcome]] required the spirit to remain until permission was given. The Licence closes that obligation and marks the intended end of the encounter. Its language of peace between speaker and spirit contrasts with the conditional threats used to compel an appearance. The closing rubric then assigns thanksgiving to [[God (Goetia)|God]] for the favourable outcome and protection. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=67|Conditions of the Welcome, PDF 67]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=68|Dismissal and thanksgiving, PDF 68]].

The text still speaks through an unnamed operator to spirit N. It supplies no date, location, or eyewitness account of a specific dismissal. These are prescribed roles in the early modern ritual, presented in the edition's archaic English. See [[Goetia Historical Context]].

## The note about the brass vessel

The paragraph after the dismissal mentions the brass vessel as an alternative place of appearance to the triangle. This is an additional ceremonial variant, not evidence that the licence itself imprisons every departing spirit. The separate [[Goetia Angelic-Language Conjurations|angelic-language version]] also ends with a dismissal.
