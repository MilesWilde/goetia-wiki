# Divine figures, names, and ritual identities

The invocations contain divine names, epithets, magical words, and identities assumed by the speaker. A string used in a prayer does not by itself establish a separate being. This page groups them by passage and distinguishes the invocation's text from its later annotations.

[[Goetia Ritual Names]] explains the better-understood titles and formulas, including [[Emmanuel (Goetia)|Emmanuel]], and distinguishes those meanings from uncertain spellings and ritual vocabulary. Follow [[Goetia Conjurations]] for the purposes of the ceremonies in which they occur.

## God and the names of authority

The conjurations appeal to a supreme creator, called **[[God (Goetia)|God]]**, **Lord**, **Most High**, and by many names. They portray that authority as superior to the spirits and their kings. The Rosicrucian formula in the preface also names **[[Jesus (Goetia)|Jesus]]** and the **[[Holy Spirit (Goetia)|Holy Spirit]]**. ## Seals, triangles, and the speaker's name

**[[Agla (Goetia)|Agla]], [[Tetragrammaton (Goetia)|Tetragrammaton]], Tau, [[Alpha and Omega (Goetia)|Alpha]], [[Alpha and Omega (Goetia)|Omega]], Abdia, Ballaton, Halliza, and Soluzen** appear in the pentagram/hexagram/ring discussion and diagrams. The later prayer gives **Bellator / Ballaton**, **Bellonoy / Bellony**, **Hallii**, and **Hra**. For the triangle it gives **[[Anaphaxeton (Goetia)|Anephezeton]]**, **[[Primeumaton (Goetia)|Primeumaton]]**, and **[[Michael (Goetia)|Michael]]**; the earlier triangle passage also notes **[[Anaphaxeton (Goetia)|Anepheneton]]** as a form of **[[Anaphaxeton (Goetia)|Anaphaxeton]]**. These are diagram names and interpretive prayers, not enough evidence for a separate character each. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=52|PDF 52–55]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=71|71]].

**[[Octinomos (Goetia)|Octinomos]]** is a name or title asserted by the **exorcist** in the [[Goetia Address and Welcome|address to the spirit]]. **Bathal / Vathat**, **Abrac**, **Abeor**, and **Aberer** occur in the following formula. [[Aleister Crowley (Goetia)|Crowley]]'s note disputes their treatment as names of [[God (Goetia)|God]]; the electronic editor discusses variants including **Bathat** and **Vachat**. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=67|PDF 67]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=84|84]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=96|96]].

The [[Goetia Angelic-Language Conjurations|Angelic-language section]] translates the conjurations rather than adding another spirit catalogue. Words such as **Iaida** and **Iada** serve its language of divine authority; **roré**, **geraa**, and the other rank terms are discussed as vocabulary in the editorial notes. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=75|PDF 75–85]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=97|97]].
