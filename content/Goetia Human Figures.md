# Human and legendary figures

These names belong to the book's framing stories, biblical precedents, ritual identities, or editorial history. They are not additional members of the 72-spirit catalogue. The entries describe their role in this PDF rather than attempting independent biographies.

## Solomon and the book's legendary transmission

| Figure | Role in the text | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **[[Solomon (Goetia)\|Solomon]] / [[Solomon (Goetia)\|King Solomon]]** | Attributed master of the spirits and recipient of sacred knowledge. The vessel story credits him with binding the 72; the introduction credits [[Michael (Goetia)\|Michael]] with revealing notes to him. | [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=21\|PDF 21–24]], [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=49\|49]] |
| **[[Lemuel (Goetia)\|Lemuel]]** | Named as [[Solomon (Goetia)\|Solomon]]'s son and recipient of the book in the heading to the Angelic-language version. | [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=73\|PDF 73]] |
| **An unnamed Jewish Rabbi** | A manuscript-origin story credits him with finding books in Jerusalem and translating them. No personal name is supplied. | [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=24\|PDF 24]] |
| **[[Ham (Goetia)\|Cham]] / [[Ham (Goetia)\|Ham]], son of [[Noah (Goetia)\|Noah]]** | Mentioned in the electronic editor's account of [[Wier (Goetia)\|Wier]]'s digression about [[Gäap]]: the first post-Flood human invoker of evil spirits in that story. This is a report of a comparative source, not an extra Goetia profile. | [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=93\|PDF 93]] |
| **[[Noah (Goetia)\|Noah]]** | Named in that same account as [[Ham (Goetia)\|Ham]]'s father. | [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=93\|PDF 93]] |
