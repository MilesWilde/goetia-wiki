# Goetia Conjurations

The conjurations are the book's **scripts for calling a selected spirit, establishing authority over it, requesting its services, and ending the encounter**. They are a major section because the catalogue tells the reader *which* spirits supposedly do particular things, while the ritual supplies the words and framework for approaching them. These notes explain the text's intentions; they do not assert that its promised effects occurred. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=60|Main ritual, PDF 60–68]]; [[Goetia Offices and Motifs]].

## Reading the sequence

The ordinary outline is preparation → summons → appearance and requests → dismissal. The constraints and curses are **conditional responses to failure**, not mandatory stages through which every encounter passes. The text itself offers different explanations for a spirit's absence and different recourses. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=63|Conditional rubrics, PDF 63–66]].

| Section | What it is intended to accomplish | Place in the text |
|---|---|---|
| [[Goetia Preliminary Invocation\|Preliminary Invocation]] | Ask the Bornless One for general spiritual authority | Modern preliminary, with an ancient source; separate from the English summons sequence |
| [[Goetia Preparation and Vestments\|Preparation and Vestments]] | Establish purity, protection, and the operator's claimed authority | Preparation before the selected spirit is addressed |
| [[Goetia First Conjuration\|First Conjuration]] | Call the spirit into a peaceful, intelligible appearance | Initial summons |
| [[Goetia Second Conjuration\|Second Conjuration]] | Reinforce the summons through sacred names and biblical precedents | Offered if the first summons has not succeeded |
| [[Goetia Constraint\|Constraint]] | Threaten consequences for continued refusal | Further escalation; introduces alternative explanations for failure |
| [[Goetia Invocation of the King\|Invocation of the King]] | Require the spirit's superior to send it | Conditional appeal to the relevant ruler |
| [[Goetia Spirits Chain\|Spirits' Chain / General Curse]] | Break alleged impediments and threaten binding for disobedience | Further conditional compulsion |
| [[Goetia Conjuration of Fire\|Conjuration of Fire]] | Make fire an instrument of threatened punishment | Material coercion involving the spirit's written name or seal |
| [[Goetia Greater Curse\|Greater Curse]] | Intensify that threat to loss of office and punitive confinement | Final major escalation if the spirit still refuses |
| [[Goetia Address and Welcome\|Address and Welcome]] | Maintain control while courteously receiving the spirit and stating requests | After an appearance, whichever summons brought it about |
| [[Goetia Licence to Depart\|Licence to Depart]] | Release the spirit peacefully and close the encounter | Conclusion |
| [[Goetia Explanation of Names\|Explanation of Certain Names]] | Associate sacred inscriptions with prayers and intended functions | Supplementary explanation, not a new encounter |
| [[Goetia Angelic-Language Conjurations\|Angelic-Language Conjurations]] | Recast much of the ritual in a different sacred vocabulary | Modern parallel version, not a continuation of the English sequence |

Each linked note gives the relevant PDF pages and distinguishes the ritual wording from later commentary.

## Who is involved?

The principal human role is an **unnamed operator**, sometimes called an exorcist or master. **N. means the chosen spirit's name**: it is a variable in the formula, not another being. The spirit is asked to appear and perform work appropriate to its attributed office. The [[Goetia Invocation of the King|king]] may be addressed as its superior; [[God (Goetia)|God]] is invoked as the authority above both. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=60|Name-placeholder note, PDF 60]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=64|Hierarchy, PDF 64]].

[[Solomon (Goetia)|Solomon]] provides the legendary authority for the book and its objects. [[Moses (Goetia)|Moses]] and other biblical figures appear as precedents for divine power. They are not participants standing beside the operator in a single historical ceremony. Similarly, [[Emmanuel (Goetia)|Emmanuel]] functions as a sacred name in an appeal to deliverance; its presence does not imply a numbered spirit with an appearance, legion count, and biography. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|Biblical precedents, PDF 61–63]].

## When would these have occurred?

The headings are **prescriptions, not dated event records**. The main English ritual belongs to the early modern Goetia tradition, while this PDF layers a 1904 edition and a 2003 reset over it. The Preliminary Invocation has an ancient Greco-Egyptian source but a modern place in this book; the angelic-language versions are also modern additions. [[Goetia Historical Context]] separates the surviving witnesses, contributors, and dates instead of assigning every section to Solomon's lifetime.
