# Goetia Historical Context

The conjurations in this PDF do not all belong to one period. The main Goetia is an **early modern English compilation attributed to Solomon**; the edition also includes a modern adaptation of an ancient prayer, modern angelic-language versions, and twentieth- and twenty-first-century editorial material. Dating those texts is different from dating a ceremony supposedly performed with them. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=5|Edition title page, PDF 5]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=89|Preliminary Invocation sources, PDF 89]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=91|Compilation discussion, PDF 91]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=97|Angelic-language versions, PDF 97]].

## Who actually took part?

Three kinds of people must be kept separate.

- **Roles inside the ritual:** an unnamed operator addresses the chosen spirit N., sometimes appeals to its king, and invokes divine authority. These formulas tell a prospective practitioner what to say; they do not identify the practitioner who actually used a particular copy. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=60|Ritual roles, PDF 60–68]].
- **Authorities invoked in the speech:** Solomon, [[Moses (Goetia)|Moses]], [[David (Goetia)|David]], and other scriptural figures lend precedent and legitimacy. Their names are not eyewitness signatures or proof that they composed these passages. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=60|Preparatory prayer, PDF 60]]; [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=61|Biblical precedents, PDF 61–63]].
- **People involved in transmission:** [[Wier (Goetia)|Weyer]], [[Scot (Goetia)|Scot]], [[Robert Turner (Goetia)|Turner]], [[Mathers (Goetia)|S. L. MacGregor Mathers]], and [[Aleister Crowley (Goetia)|Aleister Crowley]] belong to the history of copying, publishing, translating, adapting, or editing the material. Their involvement in a publication does not establish that each performed every ritual it contains. Sources for their particular contributions are given in the timeline.

Dee's surviving conference records are a different sort of evidence from a generic ritual script: they record claimed experiences on named dates. They do **not** establish that Dee and Kelly performed the Goetia conjurations later recast by Crowley. The distinction is between a record of someone's reported practice and an instruction intended for any reader. [British Library, Sloane MS 3188](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002115572); [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=97|Later adaptation, PDF 97]].

## What “Solomonic” means here

The book presents its authority as descending from Solomon. Historically, the surviving compilation joins material from different sources and periods. A claim of ancient authorship is therefore part of the text's religious and literary framework, rather than a secure attribution to the biblical king. The evidence does not provide a date on which Solomon performed these extant English formulas. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=91|Composite source history, PDF 91]]; [Peterson's introduction and manuscript comparisons](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm).

The PDF also credits Mathers in the language of translation, while its electronic editor notes that the relevant *Lemegeton* manuscripts are already English. His editorial work should not be imagined as a straightforward translation from a single surviving ancient Hebrew original. [[sources/ac_goetia.pdf#page=88|Electronic editor on the translation credit, PDF 88]].
